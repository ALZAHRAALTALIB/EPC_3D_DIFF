## Abstract

Cone-beam CT (CBCT) is routinely acquired during radiotherapy for patient setup, but its quantitative reliability is degraded by scatter, noise, and reconstruction artifacts, limiting Hounsfield Unit (HU) accuracy. We propose **EPC-3D-Diff**, a novel conditional 3D latent diffusion framework for volumetric CBCT-to-CT synthesis that introduces a **projection-domain equivariance loss** derived from acquisition physics. Unlike common image-domain equivariance, we exploit the fact that an in-plane rotation of the volume corresponds to an angular shift in its projections. During training, we enforce this relationship by forward-projecting rotated synthesized CT volumes and matching them to appropriately angle-shifted projections of the paired target CT, yielding a physics-consistent equivariance constraint integrated into the diffusion objective. To capture full 3D context efficiently, conditional diffusion is performed in a compact latent space learnt by a lightweight 3D autoencoder, preserving axial depth while downsampling in-plane resolution for stable training.

We validate on a paired head CBCT/CT phantom dataset, including repeat scans, and paired clinical data using patient-wise splits, and perform single- and mixed-domain training, ablations, and comparisons with diffusion and CycleGAN baselines. EPC-3D-Diff generalizes well and achieves substantial improvements — **+7.4 dB (phantom)** and **+1.8 dB (clinical data)** in PSNR compared to state-of-the-art methods — alongside improved SSIM and HU accuracy within tissue boundaries. Overall, EPC-3D-Diff improves robustness and physics consistency, supporting HU-aware synthesis for downstream radiotherapy workflows.

</HighlightedSection>

## Why this matters

CT provides the anatomical and electron-density information that radiotherapy planning and dose calculation depend on. CBCT is acquired at treatment time for patient setup because it's fast and low-dose, but scatter and cone-beam artifacts corrupt its Hounsfield Units — exactly the quantity dose calculations rely on. Turning daily CBCT into CT-quality volumes ("synthetic CT") would let clinics re-plan treatment using up-to-date anatomy, without an extra CT scan. The catch: most learned CBCT-to-CT methods are trained slice-by-slice or with generic image-domain regularizers, and nothing forces the output to stay consistent with how CT and CBCT scanners actually acquire data.

<TwoColumns>
<Fragment slot="left">

**The core idea.** EPC-3D-Diff adds a training-time physics constraint that is specific to tomographic imaging: rotating a 3D volume in-plane is *equivalent* to angularly shifting its own projections. The model enforces this identity between its own synthesized output and the real CT acquisition geometry — not just between the output and a generic augmented copy of itself, which is what conventional image-domain equivariant imaging does.

</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="right">

**Why 3D latent diffusion.** Working directly in voxel space at full resolution is memory-prohibitive in 3D. A lightweight 3D autoencoder compresses each volume into a compact latent (downsampling in-plane while preserving axial depth), and a conditional diffusion U-Net denoises in that latent space, conditioned on the encoded CBCT.

</Fragment>

Let $\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_c \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times M \times S}$ denote the CT and CBCT aligned 3D volumes, and $\mathbf{A}_0$, $\mathbf{A}_c$ the discretised helical-CT and cone-beam forward operators. A lightweight 3D autoencoder $(E_\psi, D_\omega)$ compresses volumes into a latent space; a conditional 3D U-Net $\varepsilon_\theta$ is trained with a standard denoising-diffusion objective, conditioned on the encoded CBCT latent $z_c = E_\psi(\mathbf{x}_c)$.

On top of this, the **projection-domain equivariance loss** exploits a simple geometric fact: rotating a volume by an in-plane angle $\varphi$ is equivalent to shifting its projections by $\varphi$ in the angular (view) coordinate. During training, the network's own denoised estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0$ is rotated by $R_\varphi$, forward-projected with $\mathbf{A}_0$, and compared against the angle-shifted projections $T_\varphi(\mathbf{y}_0)$ of the *paired ground-truth CT* — not a synthetic augmentation of the output itself:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{eq}} = \big\| \mathbf{A}_0\, R_\varphi(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0) - T_\varphi(\mathbf{y}_0) \big\|_2^2 , \qquad \mathbf{y}_0 = \mathbf{A}_0 \mathbf{x}_0 .
$$

This differs from conventional image-domain equivariant imaging [@chen2021equivariant], which only checks self-consistency between a network's output and its own transformed input, with no ground truth and no connection to the physical acquisition process. Anchoring the constraint in projection space, where rotation and angular shift are *exactly* equivalent by construction, ties training directly to how CT and CBCT data are actually acquired. $\mathcal{L}_{\text{eq}}$ is combined with the latent diffusion denoising loss and a raw-data-consistency term into the total training objective.

Models are evaluated under both **single-domain** training (trained and tested within one dataset) and **mixed-domain** training (trained jointly across phantom and clinical data), against CycleGAN [@liang2019generating] and conditional-DDPM [@peng2024cbctbased] baselines.

## Takeaways

- Anchoring an equivariance constraint in **projection space**, where a rotation of the volume is *exactly* an angular shift of its own acquisition geometry, ties training to physics in a way that ordinary image-domain augmentation consistency cannot.
- Performing conditional diffusion in a **compact 3D latent space** makes full-volume (rather than slice-wise) CBCT-to-CT synthesis computationally tractable.
- The largest gains appear under **mixed-domain training**, where the physics constraint appears to act as a stabilizing signal shared across phantom and clinical data distributions.
- Gains transfer from a controlled phantom to real **clinical data**, supporting the case for HU-aware synthetic CT in downstream radiotherapy re-planning workflows.
