## Abstract

Cone-beam CT (CBCT) is routinely acquired during radiotherapy for patient setup, but its quantitative reliability is degraded by scatter, noise, and reconstruction artifacts, limiting Hounsfield Unit (HU) accuracy. We propose **EPC-3D-Diff**, a conditional 3D latent diffusion framework for volumetric CBCT-to-CT synthesis that introduces a **projection-domain equivariance loss** derived from acquisition physics. Unlike common image-domain equivariance, the proposed training objective exploits the fact that an in-plane rotation of a 3D volume corresponds to an angular shift of its projections. During training, rotated synthesized CT volumes are forward-projected and matched to appropriately shifted projections of the paired target CT, yielding a physics-consistent constraint integrated into the diffusion objective.

To capture full volumetric context efficiently, diffusion is performed in a compact latent space learned by a lightweight 3D autoencoder. The method is evaluated on paired head-and-neck phantom and clinical CBCT/CT datasets under both single-domain and mixed-domain training, with comparisons against diffusion and CycleGAN baselines. EPC-3D-Diff achieves substantial improvements, including **+7.4 dB PSNR on the phantom cohort** and **+1.8 dB PSNR on the clinical cohort**, together with improved SSIM and HU fidelity.

</SectionPanel>

## 1. Introduction

CT provides the anatomical and electron-density information required for radiotherapy planning and dose calculation, whereas CBCT is typically acquired during treatment for image guidance and patient setup. However, CBCT is affected by scatter, beam-hardening, truncation, and reconstruction artifacts, leading to unreliable HU values and reduced soft-tissue fidelity.

EPC-3D-Diff addresses this CBCT-to-CT synthesis problem by combining **3D latent conditional diffusion** with a **physics-consistent projection-domain equivariance loss**. The resulting framework is designed to improve anatomical fidelity, HU accuracy, and robustness across data domains and scanner settings.

### Key insight

For circular acquisition geometry, an **in-plane rotation** of the synthesized CT corresponds to an **angular shift** in projection space. EPC-3D-Diff enforces this relationship during training, so the model learns not only to match CT appearance in image space, but also to remain consistent with the underlying acquisition physics.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Problem setup

Let $\mathbf{x}_0$ and $\mathbf{x}_c$ denote the aligned CT and CBCT 3D volumes, respectively, and let $\mathbf{A}_0$ and $\mathbf{A}_c$ denote the discretized helical-CT and cone-beam forward operators. A lightweight 3D autoencoder $(E_\psi, D_\omega)$ maps the volumes into a compact latent space:

$$
z_0 = E_\psi(\mathbf{x}_0), \qquad z_c = E_\psi(\mathbf{x}_c).
$$

A conditional 3D U-Net $\varepsilon_\theta$ is then trained to predict the injected diffusion noise in latent space, conditioned on the encoded CBCT representation $z_c$.

### 2.2 Projection-domain equivariance loss

The central idea of EPC-3D-Diff is that an in-plane rotation of the synthetic CT should correspond to an angular shift of its projections. During training, the denoised estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0$ is rotated by $R_\varphi$, forward-projected with $\mathbf{A}_0$, and compared against the correspondingly shifted projections of the paired target CT:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eq}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_\varphi}
\left\| T_{\varphi_i}(\mathbf{y}_0) - \mathbf{A}_0 R_{\varphi_i}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0) \right\|_2^2,
\qquad
\mathbf{y}_0 = \mathbf{A}_0\mathbf{x}_0.
$$

This constraint is integrated with the diffusion loss and the complementary image-domain losses to form the training objective. Importantly, the projection operators are required **only during training**; at inference time, sampling is performed efficiently from the learned conditional prior using the CBCT input alone.

### 3.1 Datasets and implementation details

<Fragment slot="left">

**NWH phantom dataset.**

8 patients for training and 2 patients for testing.

</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="right">

**JUST clinical dataset.**  
Paired head-and-neck CBCT/CT data from King Abdullah University Hospital (Jordan), with patient-wise train/test splits.

**Split used in the paper.**  
11 patients for training and 3 patients for testing.

</Fragment>
</TwoColumns>

All images are converted to HU, clipped to the fixed window $[-1000, 2000]$, normalized to $[-1,1]$, and resized to $256 \times 256$. The latent autoencoder uses 4 channels, the conditional 3D U-Net uses a base width of 64, and diffusion training is performed with 1000 timesteps and DDIM inference with 100 sampling steps.

## 4. Conclusion

EPC-3D-Diff combines 3D latent conditional diffusion with a projection-domain equivariance constraint derived from CT acquisition physics. By enforcing rotational consistency in projection space during training, the method improves HU fidelity and robustness under mixed-domain learning and scanner shifts, while preserving efficient operator-free inference at test time.
